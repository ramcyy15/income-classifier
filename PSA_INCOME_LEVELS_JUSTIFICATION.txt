PHILIPPINE STATISTICS AUTHORITY (PSA) & PIDS INCOME TIER CLASSIFICATION
JUSTIFICATION AND FORMULA EXPLANATION FOR THESIS DEFENSE
================================================================================

1. WHAT IS THE OFFICIAL PHILIPPINE GOVERNMENT STANDARD?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In the Philippines, the official government standard for income and poverty
classification is defined by the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) and
the Philippine Institute for Development Studies (PIDS) under the National
Economic and Development Authority (NEDA).

The foundational formula is based on the official Poverty Threshold (PT).
- Poverty Threshold (PT): The minimum amount required per capita to satisfy
  basic food (2,000 kcal/day nutritional requirement) and non-food needs
  (shelter, clothing, utilities, health, and education).
- According to official PSA FIES data, the average monthly poverty threshold
  for a standard Filipino family of 5 is approximately PHP 12,000 to PHP 13,800.


2. HOW DID WE COME UP WITH LEVEL 1, LEVEL 2, AND LEVEL 3?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Officially, PIDS and PSA define seven (7) specific income brackets. Because
training a machine learning model on 7 fragmented micro-categories causes severe
data sparsity and low generalization, researchers and government economists
(Albert et al., PIDS Policy Study) officially cluster them into three (3)
macro socio-economic tiers:

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TIER LABEL      OFFICIAL PSA / PIDS SUB-CLASSES        FORMULA & THRESHOLD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LEVEL 1         • Poor                                 Income < 2 x Poverty Line
(Low-Income)    • Low-Income (but not poor)            (< PHP 24,000 / month)

LEVEL 2         • Lower Middle-Income Class            2 x to 12 x Poverty Line
(Middle-Income) • Middle Middle-Income Class           (PHP 24,000 - PHP 140,000)
                • Upper Middle-Income Class

LEVEL 3         • Upper-Income Class (not rich)        Income > 12 x Poverty Line
(High-Income)   • Rich / High-Income Class             (> PHP 140,000 / month)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Citation for Paper / Defense:
Albert, J. R. G., Santos, A. G. F., & Vizmanos, J. F. V. "Defining and
Profiling the Middle Class." Philippine Institute for Development Studies (PIDS).


3. HOW DOES A HOUSEHOLD FORMULA APPLY TO A BARANGAY COMMUNITY?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question: "The PSA formula is for a household. How did you apply it to a barangay?"

Answer:
A barangay does not earn a single salary; a barangay is an aggregate community of
households. Therefore, we use two official statistical principles:

1. COMMUNITY AGGREGATE INCOME (PSA FIES):
   - The barangay's economic tier is determined by its Average Monthly Household
     Income (avg_monthly_income_php).
   - If the average household in that barangay earns below 2x Poverty Threshold,
     the community has a dominant Low-Income profile.

2. PSA MULTIDIMENSIONAL POVERTY INDEX (MPI) INDICATORS:
   Income alone can be misleading. Therefore, the model assesses 9 additional
   community-level indicators from the PSA Census of Population and Housing (CPH)
   and Labor Force Survey (LFS):
   - Informal Settlement Rate (%)
   - Access to Electricity (%)
   - Access to Safe Drinking Water (%)
   - Sanitary Toilet Coverage (%)
   - Permanent Wall & Roof Housing Materials (%)
   - Youth Net School Enrollment Rate (%)
   - High School Education Attainment (%)
   - Household Head Employment Rate (%)
   - Average Family Size (members)

SUMMARY DEFINITION:
- Level 1 Barangay: Community where average income is below 2x Poverty Line,
  characterized by high informal settlement rates and deficits in basic utilities.
- Level 2 Barangay: Community with transitional income (2x to 12x Poverty Line),
  moderate utility coverage, and growing educational attainment.
- Level 3 Barangay: Economically developed community with high average income,
  near-complete utility integration, permanent housing, and low deprivation.
